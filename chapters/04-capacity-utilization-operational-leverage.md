# Chapter 4: Capacity Utilization & Extreme Operational Leverage

## 4.1 Fixed vs. Variable Cost Architecture
A semiconductor fab possesses one of the highest fixed-cost structures in modern capitalism:
- Fixed Costs ($75\%–85\%$ of total): Tool depreciation, building debt service, cleanroom HVAC power, baseline engineering staff.
- Variable Costs ($15\%–25\%$ of total): Raw silicon wafers ($$150\text{–}\$300/wafer$), specialty chemicals, electricity, consumables.

## 4.2 The Operating Margin Seesaw
Because fixed costs are incurred whether the fab runs 10 wafers or 100,000 wafers, changes in capacity utilization have a leveraged impact on profitability:

$$\Delta \text{Operating Profit} = \Delta \text{Revenue} \times \left( \frac{\text{Price} - \text{Variable Cost}}{\text{Price}} \right)$$

- At **$70\%$ Utilization:** Revenue fails to cover fixed depreciation; operating margin collapses to near zero or net losses.
- At **$85\%$ Utilization:** Fab covers fixed overhead; operating margin hits $25\%$.
- At **$98\%$ Utilization:** Every incremental wafer sold carries an **$80\%+$ marginal contribution margin**, driving company-wide operating margins above $45\%$.
