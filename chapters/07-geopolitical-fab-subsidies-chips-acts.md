# Chapter 7: Geopolitical Subsidies: The Economics of Global CHIPS Acts

## 7.1 The Geopolitical Fracture
Historically, semiconductor manufacturing concentrated in East Asia (Taiwan, South Korea, Japan) due to dense engineering supply chains and $30\%–50\%$ lower operating costs. Post-2020 supply chain shocks catalyzed national security legislation:
- **US CHIPS Act:** $52.7\text{B}$ in direct manufacturing subsidies and investment tax credits.
- **European Chips Act:** $€43\text{B}$ public/private funding package.
- **Japan Semiconductor Strategy:** Multi-trillion yen grants funding TSMC Kumamoto (JASM) and Rapidus.

## 7.2 The Cost Disadvantage of Western Fabs
Building and operating fabs in the US and Europe carries severe structural cost headwinds:
- **Construction Capex:** A fab in Arizona costs **$3\times \text{ to } 4\times$ more** to build than an identical fab in Tainan, Taiwan, due to union labor, regulatory permitting, and lack of domestic construction expertise.
- **Operating OpEx:** Higher labor costs and fragmented local supplier networks create a permanent **$20\%–30\%$ unit cost penalty** per wafer.
- **Subsidies as Capital Buffers:** Government grants bridge the initial capex gap, but long-term fab survival requires foundries to charge premium wafer prices to defense and hyperscaler customers demanding onshore supply chains.
