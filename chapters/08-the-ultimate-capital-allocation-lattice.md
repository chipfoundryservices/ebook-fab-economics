# Chapter 8: The Ultimate Capital Allocation Lattice in Semiconductors

## 8.1 The Munger Inversion Applied to Chip Stocks
Applying first-principles capital allocation to semiconductor investments:
- *Invert: What destroys capital in semiconductors?*
  1. Competing in commodity memory (DRAM/NAND) without structural cost leadership.
  2. Running a fab below $80\%$ capacity utilization.
  3. Attempting to build an in-house leading-edge fab without the scale of TSMC's customer base (e.g., Intel's multi-billion-dollar foundry losses).

## 8.2 The ROIC Hierarchy Across the Value Chain
Over full economic cycles, economic value pools in specific segments of the semiconductor value chain:
1. **Monopolistic Equipment Vendors (ASML, KLA, Lam, AMAT):** $30\%–42\%$ ROIC. Pure intellectual property, recurring service annuities, low capital requirements relative to revenue.
2. **Pure-Play Foundry Monopolist (TSMC):** $20\%–26\%$ ROIC. Immense capital expenditures ($$30B+/year$) offset by monopolistic pricing power, $>50\%$ gross margins, and customer co-investment.
3. **Leading-Edge Fabless Designers (Nvidia, Apple):** $40\%–60\%+$ ROIC. Zero capital expenditure, high software-integrated gross margins ($75\%+$).
4. **Sub-Scale Foundries & Commodity IDMs:** $5\%–12\%$ ROIC. Sub-scale capex, constant price wars, and under-utilized fab assets destroy shareholder capital.
