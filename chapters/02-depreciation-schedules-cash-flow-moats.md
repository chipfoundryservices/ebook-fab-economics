# Chapter 2: Depreciation Schedules & Cash Flow Generation Moats

## 2.1 Accounting Depreciation vs. Economic Reality
In standard financial accounting, semiconductor fabrication equipment is depreciated using the straight-line method over a **5-year useful life**:

$$\text{Annual Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Initial Equipment Capex}}{5}$$

For a fab with $15\text{B}$ in tools, annual depreciation expense is **$3.0\text{B per year}$ ($$250\text{M/month}$)**.
During Years 1 through 5, this non-cash expense depresses reported accounting net income.

## 2.2 The Depreciated Asset Goldmine
In physical reality, high-quality semiconductor tools **do not expire after 5 years**. Tools operate reliably for **15 to 25+ years**:
- In Year 6, depreciation drops to zero.
- The fab continues producing millions of revenue-generating wafers with zero equipment depreciation charge.
- TSMC's mature-node fabs (Fab 12, Fab 14) running 28nm, 40nm, and 65nm operate as massive, unlevered cash-flow machines, generating **operating margins exceeding $60\%$** and funding the next generation of leading-edge GigaFabs.
